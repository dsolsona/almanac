## Outcome and non-goals

A due boundary whose canonical cutoff predates its immutable policy revision's
activation records an explicit ineligible range and advances atomically. It must
neither pin forever nor produce an evaluation/occurrence. This remains an unwired
processor change. It does not activate the runtime, adopt the proposed maintenance
calculator, add source-edit fanout/archive, or waive scaled release gates.

Activation limits the evaluation cutoff, not observation timestamps inside full
windows. Existing SLO/SLI validity and retention still govern those populations.

## Acceptance and expected cases

An existing SLO has sufficient eligible measurements. A policy activated at
12:00:10 has a 12:01 due boundary whose canonical cutoff is 12:00. Processing
records exactly `[12:01,12:02)` as before-policy-activation work, clears the pin,
leaves failure attempts unchanged and creates no numerical evidence or alert.
The next eligible cutoff can fire using historical observations in its full
windows; it need not wait a policy lifetime equal to the long window.

Real PostgreSQL and explicit-clock regressions must establish:

- A later eligible cutoff evaluates normally, including equality at activation.
- A sub-minute source cutoff between activation and rounded cursor origin is
  eligible. Existing pre-activation pins are skipped on the next attempt.
- Lease expiry, disable and semantic replacement fence stale skip/advance work.
- Direct canonical preparation/journal publication cannot bypass eligibility.
- Enable/semantic edits use fresh activation; metadata preserves activation.
- Pinned pending work, restart, repeat/catch-up ordering and fairness retain their
  existing behavior. Adjust unrealistic fixture activation clocks explicitly;
  never weaken assertions to accept a pre-activation numerical decision.
- Persisted range reads, immutable chart scheduled provenance and both clients
  recognize the new kind without describing it as missing datasource evidence.

User acceptance is a protected API/CLI policy with an existing SLO, followed by
an explicit isolated processor turn. History shows the skipped scheduled work;
the later eligible turn creates the ordinary occurrence. Production registration
and sustained capacity acceptance remain separate. No new UI controls are needed.
